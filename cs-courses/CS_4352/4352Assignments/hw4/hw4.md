# Assignment 4 – ATM User Segmentation & Persona

## Step 1: Splitting ATM Users into Groups

After looking at the wide range of ATM users listed in the table, I thought about people I know in real life. That helped me break them down into two main groups: 1. Group 1: People like my mom
These are middle-aged adults living and working in America who aren’t super comfortable with English or technology. They use ATMs when they have to, but they prefer it to be simple and clear. They’re usually just trying to get in and out quickly without messing anything up. 2. Group 2: Tech-savvy young adults like me
These are students or young professionals who are really comfortable with tech but get frustrated fast if something feels clunky or poorly designed. We expect things to be fast, modern, and intuitive — and if it’s not, we’re out.

For this assignment, I’m focusing on Group 1 — people like my mom. I think this group is often overlooked when it comes to technology, especially in a country where English isn’t their first language. They still use ATMs regularly, but their needs are totally different from people who grew up with smartphones.

## Step 2: Persona for Group 1

Name: Rosa Hernandez
Age: 46
Background: Rosa works full-time at a local bakery and has lived in the U.S. for over 15 years. She understands some English when it’s spoken, but reading and writing in English is still a challenge. She doesn’t use a computer often and doesn’t own a smartphone — just a basic cell phone.
Tech comfort level: Very low. She only uses the ATM when she needs to withdraw cash or check her balance. She gets nervous she might press the wrong button or mess something up.
Motivation: Rosa likes using ATMs because they’re faster than waiting in line at the bank. But only if she understands what to do.
Attitude toward ATMs: She’s hesitant and cautious. If the ATM has too many screens, small text, or unclear instructions, she’ll avoid using it and just wait for help inside the bank.
Needs from an ATM:
• Clear, simple instructions (preferably with a Spanish option)
• Big buttons and readable text
• Fewer screens and options
• Visual cues or icons to guide her
• A “go back” option if she gets confused

---

## Why This Persona Matters

People like Rosa are common in the U.S., especially in immigrant communities. A lot of ATM interfaces seem to assume the user is fluent in English and comfortable with digital systems. But Rosa just wants to check her balance or get $60 in cash — without getting overwhelmed. Designing with her in mind means making the ATM experience less intimidating, more visual, and easier to navigate without relying too much on text.

### What Are Storyboards?

- Graphical tools that use a sequence of illustrations to visualize a story
- Similar to comic strips - include people and their needs
- Visual storytelling process that helps predict and explore user experiences
- Based on scenarios (descriptions of possible sequences of events)
- Don't require artistic ability - focus is on communicating ideas
- Follow the need-finding process when developing user-centered designs

### Benefits of Storyboards

- **Visualization**: Pictures communicate more effectively than words
- **Memorability**: Stories are 22 times more memorable than plain facts
- **Empathy**: Help people relate to and empathize with users
- **Engagement**: Stories naturally capture attention
- **Human-centered**: Involve designers, stakeholders, and end-users
- **User perspective**: Force designers to think from the user's point of view
- **Team collaboration**: Enable pitch and critique methods
- **Early mistake detection**: Inexpensive and quick compared to software development

### Origin and Examples

- Originated in the film industry
- Invented by Disney Studios in the 1930s
- Used in films like Star Wars and The Lion King
- Each panel represents one scene or important interaction

### Purpose and Application

- Primary purpose is communication, not artistic illustration
- Helps formulate scenarios from the user's perspective
- Used early in the design process before interface specifics are determined
- Connects user needs identified in need-finding to potential solutions

### Creating Scenarios

- Scenarios are the foundation of storyboards, containing four key elements:
  - Character: The persona with behaviors, expectations, feelings, and decisions
  - Scene: The environment where the story takes place
  - Plot: Begins with a trigger event and ends with a benefit/solution or problem
  - Narrative: Focuses on the character's goal with a clear beginning, middle, and end

### Storyboard Development Process

- Start with plain text and arrows to outline the story moments
- Add emotional states (using simple expressions) to each step
- Translate each step into illustrated storyboard frames
- Example demonstrated: Tom suffering from depression and Susan using the Heartline app to check on him

### Five Visual Elements of Storytelling

- Level of detail: Less detailed illustrations are more universal (cartoon faces versus realistic faces)
- Inclusion of text: Keep text short but sufficient to explain the scenario
- Inclusion of people and emotions: Essential to convey the user experience
- Number of frames: Ideally 5-8 frames (maximum 15-16) to maintain focus and attention
- Portrayal of time: Include only when necessary for understanding the story

### Practical Tips

- Simple representation methods for humans (star and circle method, stick figures)
- Drawing skills aren't required - alternatives include:
  - Taking photos (blur unnecessary details)
  - Using software tools
- Examples shown: CS student planning a trip, riding a bus, finding a bus stop, and shopping at Safeway
